# Yerevan wall writings as a linguistic register

Status: **research note** — opening the topic. Captures (a) that
wall writings constitute a distinct linguistic register worth
collecting for this workspace, (b) what we have so far, (c) what
kinds of linguistic data the register surfaces.

## Why this register

Yerevan public-text walls carry data that the four-book corpus
(sakayan, ghamoyan, parnasyan, tioyan) systematically misses:

- **Spontaneous code-mixing** — script-mixing (Latin / Cyrillic /
  Armenian) within a single tag, lexical-mixing
  (Russian-in-Armenian, English-in-Russian), syntactic-mixing
  (substrate calques).
- **Colloquial / underground registers** — gangster-tag style,
  territorial markers, political slogans, romantic dedications,
  protest writing. Each has its own conventions (script choice,
  framing, voice).
- **Toponym vernacular** — district nicknames and
  street-disambiguators that don't appear in maps or news.
- **Diachronic layering** — older faded tags coexist with newer
  ones on the same wall, giving comparative-register data without
  having to source separately-dated texts.
- **Commercial / signage** — clinic signs, shop names, ad
  fly-posters; their layout and script choices shift independently
  of the graffiti.

None of this is in the source-book corpus. As a corpus of *living*
public Armenian (and Yerevan Russian) text, the four books are
deliberately curated towards literary / pedagogical / journalistic
registers. Walls fill the gap.

## What's in the workspace right now

The collection is one photograph (2026-05-14). Below the per-photo
inventory is "diverse" in the sense that the single wall carries
**four distinct layers** of writing from at least three different
registers and eras:

### `photo_5199905489704129439_y.jpg`

Wall in central Yerevan; mortared sandstone-brick façade of an
older residential building. Captured:

1. **Boxed gangster-territorial tag** (centred, most prominent):

   ```
   BRATVA
   [ KRIVO NAR-DOS ]
   ВСЕМ ПРОТИВ :)
   ```

   - Mixed-script Russian. `BRATVA` (Latin), boxed `KRIVO NAR-DOS`
     (Latin), `ВСЕМ ПРОТИВ` (Cyrillic) + smiley.
   - The boxed inner line stacks the colloquial district name
     `Krivo` onto the official street name `Nar-Dos`. See
     `2026-05-14-yerevan-district-naming.md`.
   - The closing line `ВСЕМ ПРОТИВ` carries the Armenian
     predicative `<DAT> + դեմ + (եմ)` calque (DAT `всем` +
     postposed `против`, copula elided). See
     `walks/2026-05-14-armenian-grammar-on-russian-wall.md`.

2. **Older small tag**, left of the box:

   ```
   ← АРХ 13
     BTC
   ```

   - `АРХ 13` — Cyrillic (А-Р-Х) that visually coincides with
     Latin `APX`. Script-false-friend: the reader has to commit
     to one script-reading to interpret. Yerevan-vernacular
     register would read this as Cyrillic ("Arkh"). Possibly an
     abbreviation / handle.
   - `BTC` — Bitcoin reference. Cryptocurrency-tag on a wall is
     itself a register marker — appearing more in younger /
     internet-influenced graffiti (cf. the corpus / web findings
     in `topics/lexicon/currency_exchange.md` §
     "Contemporary register: cryptocurrency").

3. **Older faded tag** (far left, partly washed):

   - `ТОМАТ` (Cyrillic) — likely a nickname / handle.
   - A second line below, illegible from the photo alone.

4. **Even older commercial signage** (very faded, ghosted onto the
   masonry):

   - `СТОМАТОЛОГИЯ` (Cyrillic, split as `СТОМАТ / ОЛОГИЯ` across
     two lines). Old painted-on dental-clinic sign — Soviet- or
     early-post-Soviet-era commercial signage. Predates the
     graffiti by likely decades.

The wall's stratigraphy goes commercial (Soviet-era, Cyrillic
clinic sign) → underground (faded `ТОМАТ`) → contemporary
script-mixed tag (`BRATVA / KRIVO NAR-DOS`). Each layer represents
a different generation of public-text register.

## What kinds of linguistic data wall photos surface

A wall photo can yield findings in any of these categories — each
worth its own walk / topic / note when material arrives:

- **Script-mixing convention** — within-piece script switches as
  register / authorship cues. The `BRATVA / ВСЕМ ПРОТИВ` switch
  inside one boxed tag is a working example.
- **Substrate / calque grammar** — Armenian L1 syntax surfacing in
  Russian L2 text. The `всем против` calque is the canonical case
  in this workspace.
- **Toponym register** — colloquial district nicknames absent from
  administrative sources. Krivo, Силачи, etc.
- **Script-false-friends** — Cyrillic strings that look Latin (or
  vice versa), exploited deliberately or ambiguously. `АРХ` /
  `APX` is the working example.
- **Loanword orthography** — non-canonical spellings for borrowed
  terms (`BTC`, `биткоин` variants). The crypto register is
  particularly productive here.
- **Generational layering** — multiple writing acts on the same
  surface, datable by paint condition, register, and content.

## How to extend this collection

When a new wall photo lands:

1. Save to workspace root (the convention so far). Filename can be
   anything — `photo_*.jpg`, `PXL_*.jpg`, etc.
2. Cite it from the relevant walks / research notes by its
   filename (workspace-relative).
3. Catalogue distinct items on the wall (each tag / sign / faded
   layer as its own sub-section), with transcribed text where
   readable.
4. Cross-link into the right topical or research artifacts
   (district-naming, contact-linguistics, code-switching,
   crypto-lexicon, etc.) rather than treating the wall photo as
   *the* artifact.

The photo is the substrate; the linguistic finding is the artifact.

## Open gaps

- Single-photo collection. Comparative register claims (e.g. "the
  Cyrillic-on-wall pattern indexes older / gangster register")
  need a second and third example to harden.
- No systematic geo-tagging of wall photos. If the collection
  grows, capturing the neighborhood / district would let toponym
  register align with geography.
- No OCR pipeline for wall photos (unlike the books). Text
  transcription is by-eye, with the risks that implies (cf. the
  initial misreading of `СТОМАТ / ОЛОГИЯ` as a nickname).
- The Yerevan-Georgian phrasebook photos (PXL_*.jpg) at workspace
  root are a *different* register (printed pedagogical text, not
  wall writing) — cited here only to note that not every
  workspace-root photo is a wall photo.
