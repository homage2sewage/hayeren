# «սաղ» — colloquial "all", and «սաղս» = "all of us"

## The lexeme `սաղ`

Colloquial Eastern Armenian, a borrowing from Ottoman Turkish صاغ
*sağ* "alive, sound, whole, right" (via Middle Armenian). Three
senses, all dialectal/colloquial:

1. **alive / unharmed** — `սաղ ես?` "are you OK?", `սաղ լինել` "to
   be alive/well" (ghamoyan p45 [#2]).
2. **whole, undamaged** — not broken or cracked.
3. **all, entire, the whole of** — `սաղ օրը` "all day"
   (ghamoyan p83 [#1]).

Sense 3 is the universal-quantifier use and the relevant one here.
It is a colloquial synonym of standard `բոլոր` / `ամբողջ` "all,
entire".

**Confidence:** well-cited for the lexeme and the "all" sense
(ghamoyan p83, running text). Catalogued as Turkish-loan colloquial
vocabulary at ghamoyan p45–46.

## `սաղս` = "all of us" — yes, and it IS deducible from Dum-Tragut

The colloquial word `սաղ` itself does **not** appear in Dum-Tragut
(it is a reference grammar of *standard* Armenian; the only `սաղ`-
substring hits there are `Երուսաղեմ` "Jerusalem" and `սաղմոս`
"psalm", i.e. noise). **But the grammatical construction is fully
described there**, so `սաղս` is deducible from the grammar even
though the word isn't in the book:

- Dum-Tragut documents the **personal suffixes** (a.k.a. possessive
  articles) `-ս / -դ / -ը` (1st/2nd/3rd person) attaching not only
  to nouns and pronouns but to **substantivized words**, including
  the universal quantifier `բոլոր` "all":
  - `բոլոր` + `-ս` → **`բոլորս`**, glossed *all.nom-my*,
    «Մենք բոլորս մեկ ընտանիք ենք» "We all are one family"
    (dumtragut printed p115 / PDF 132, ex. 158 [#3]).
  - `բոլոր` + `-դ` → **`բոլորդ`**, «Դուք բոլորդ կգաք մեր տունը»
    "You all come to our house" (printed p411 / PDF 428, ex. 271
    [#4]).
- The rule statement [#3]: *"The personal suffixes are thus used:
  with nouns and pronouns indicating a person and also with … words
  functioning like nouns…"*

Since `սաղ` (sense 3) is a colloquial synonym of `բոլոր`/`ամբողջ`
and behaves as the same substantivized universal quantifier, the
same productive rule gives:

| standard (Dum-Tragut, attested) | colloquial `սաղ` (by the same rule) |
|---|---|
| `բոլորս` "all of us" (lit. all-my) | **`սաղս`** "all of us" |
| `բոլորդ` "all of you" | **`սաղդ`** "all of you" |
| `բոլորը` "all of them / everyone" | **`սաղը`** "everyone / all" |

So `սաղս` literally parses as "սաղ (all) + -ս (1st-person personal
suffix)" = "all-my" → idiomatic **"all of us"** — structurally
identical to the attested `բոլորս`.

**Confidence on the deduction:** high. The mechanism is explicitly
described and exemplified in Dum-Tragut [#3][#4]; the only step
beyond the corpus is substituting the colloquial synonym `սաղ` for
`բոլոր`, which external lexicography confirms is licensed (below).

## External corroboration (web, not corpus)

- **English Wiktionary** independently lists the possessive forms
  **`սաղս`** (1st) and **`սաղդ`** (2nd) for `սաղ`, gives the "all,
  entire" sense, and the natural-usage example
  «դուք սաղդ դեբիլ եք» "you're all idiots" — i.e. `սաղ` + `-դ` =
  "all of you", the colloquial twin of Dum-Tragut's `բոլորդ`.
- **Armenian Wiktionary** corroborates the sense ("entire, all";
  synonyms `ամբողջ`, `բոլոր`) and the Ottoman-Turkish *sağ*
  etymology.
- **Nayiri** dictionary aggregator has the headword.

## Gaps

- The exact inflected form `սաղս` does **not** occur in any of the
  five corpus books (verified by direct grep). The "all of us"
  reading rests on: attested stem (ghamoyan) + attested productive
  rule (Dum-Tragut, on `բոլոր`) + Wiktionary's listing of the
  `սաղս/սաղդ` forms — not on a single corpus token glossed "all of
  us".
- No corpus citation glosses the string "all of us" for `սաղս`
  word-for-word; the parallel `դուք սաղդ` "all of you" (Wiktionary)
  is the closest natural-usage anchor.
