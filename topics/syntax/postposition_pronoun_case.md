# Case government of comparison postpositions `պես` and `նման`

The similative/equative postpositions `պես` "like, as" and `նման`
"like, similar to" govern their complement's case by a three-row
rule. Rows 1–2 are stated independently by two grammars
[#1][#3][#4][#5]; row 3 is inferred from the grammars' 1st/2nd
scoping plus corpus usage, which obeys it without exception in
running text (§Third person):

| complement | form | example |
|---|---|---|
| noun | gen/dat syncretic form | `ձիթապտուղի պես` "like an olive" [#2], `երեխայի պես` "like a child" [#9], `հարազատի պես` "like a relative" [#1] |
| 1st/2nd person pronoun | **dative** `ինձ, քեզ, մեզ, ձեզ` | `ձեզ պես` "like you-pl" [#4], `քեզ նման` "like you" [#11], `ինձ նման` "like me" [#9], `մեզ նման` "like us" [#14] |
| 3rd person / reflexive | genitive-shaped `նրա, իր` | `նրա պես` "like him/her/it" [#7][#8][#10], `իր հետ/մոտ` [#14] |

`պես` is the one "more often used in colloquial MEA" [#1]. Both
markers can be used predicatively [#1] — `հարազատի պես` in
Dum-Tragut's own example predicates over `սիրում է` [#1]; a
constructed first-person analogue (not corpus-attested) would be
`Քաղաքացու նման չեմ` "I'm not like a citizen."

## The rule as the grammars state it

Dum-Tragut's syntax chapter gives the blanket form: similative
markers `պես` and `նման` "co-occur with the dative on nouns and
pronouns" [#1], and the equative section repeats it — standard "in
the dative case" [#11], with `Քույրս քեզ նման գեղեցիկ է` "my sister
is as beautiful as you" (2sg dative `քեզ`) as the worked example.

Her morphology chapter carries the precise scoping in a Note: "In
combination with the **1st and 2nd person** of personal pronouns
the following postpositions and prepositions govern the dative
case" [#3] — the list includes `մոտ, վրա, հետ, պես, չափ, համար,
..., նման` with examples `ինձ մոտ, ..., ձեզ պես` [#4]. Third
person is deliberately outside the rule.

Ghamoyan states the identical norm from the prescriptive side: with
the postpositions `մոտ, վրա, հետ, պես, չափ, համար, նման, հօգուտ,
հանձին, ի դեմս, հանուն`, "first and second person personal
pronouns should be put in the dative case" [#5].

## Third person: `նրա պես`, never `նրան պես`

With 3rd-person `նա` the form before the postposition is the
genitive-shaped `նրա`, not dative `նրան`. Neither grammar states
this outright (see gaps) — it follows from the 1st/2nd scoping,
from ghamoyan's `իրեն → իր` correction in the reflexive series
[#14], and from uniform corpus usage:

- `ձիդ թող ինձ մոտ, դու էլ նրա պես` "leave your horse with me, and
  you, like him..." [#7] — dative `ինձ` and genitive-shaped `նրա`
  side by side in one clause.
- `նրա պես գիժ ու անսանձ, նրա պես հեզ ու քաղցրակարկաչ` "like it
  wild and unbridled, like it gentle and sweet-murmuring" (of a
  river) [#8].
- `իր Սասունցի Դավթի նման ն նրա պես` "like his David of Sassoun
  and like him" [#10] — with reflexive-series `իր` showing the
  same shape before a noun standard.

Four `նրա պես` tokens in running text across the five extracted
books; zero `նրան պես` / `նրան նման` anywhere [#8].

## Register: the colloquial genitive spread

In colloquial Yerevan speech the genitive invades the 1st/2nd slot:
"their use with the genitive case is very widespread: `իմ մոտ, իմ
համար, քո պես`..." [#6]. Ghamoyan files this under case-government
errors (`Խնդրառության սխալ` [#5]) and her correction table pairs
the errors with the norms: `իմ մոտ → ինձ մոտ`, `մեր նման → մեզ
նման` [#14]. So `քո պես` is street-normal but normatively wrong;
the literary norm is `քեզ պես`.

The same table corrects the reflexive series in the *opposite*
direction — `իրեն հետ, իրեն մոտ → իր հետ, իր մոտ` [#14]: colloquial
speech pushes dative-shaped forms into the genitive slot there.
Both error classes level toward a single pronoun form per
postposition, each from its own side of the person split.

## Boundary: `չափ` in the colloquial equative

The measure postposition `չափ` sits in both grammars' dative lists
— Dum-Tragut's own example is `ինձ չափ` [#4], and ghamoyan includes
`չափ` in the norm statement [#5]. But in the specifically
*colloquial equative* construction ("In colloquial Armenian there
is another construction" [#12]) Dum-Tragut records a genitive
pronoun standard: `Քույրս քո չափ գեղեցիկ է` "my sister is as
beautiful as you," `քո` glossed *gen* [#12], stated as "the
genitive (pronouns) or dative (nouns)" [#13]. The two passages are
not connected by the sources; the reading adopted here is that the
colloquial-equative genitive is the same colloquial genitive spread
ghamoyan documents [#6], recorded descriptively (see gaps). The
practical takeaway for a learner: `ինձ չափ` is the norm, `քո չափ`
is what colloquial equatives actually sound like.

## Contrastive notes

**For an English L1**: English "like X" gives no case cue at all
("like me" uses the bare object form). The trap is analogising from
the noun row (where gen and dat look identical, `երեխայի պես`) and
then picking the wrong member for pronouns, where the cases split:
1st/2nd dative (`ինձ պես`), 3rd person genitive-shaped (`նրա պես`).

**For a Russian L1**: Russian `как` + nominative / `подобно` + dative
splits by lexeme, not by person — there is no Russian analogue of a
postposition switching case by grammatical person of its complement.
Tioyan's glosses (`ինձ նման` — "как я", `երեխայի պես` — "как
ребенок") [#9] show the Russian side losing the case distinction
entirely.
