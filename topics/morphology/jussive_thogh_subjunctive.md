# Third-person jussive: `թող` + subjunctive

Armenian builds **3rd-person directives** ("let him come", "let it
stay", "let no one find out") with the particle **`թող`** + a
**subjunctive** verb. This fills the slot English fills with
*let …* and Russian fills with *пусть …* — a command or wish
addressed *about* a 3rd party, not *to* the hearer.

## Form

```
թող  +  <subjunctive verb>
```

The verb is the bare-stem subjunctive (the same form treated in
`topics/morphology/subjunctive_mood.md`), inflected for the person
and number of the *subject of the wish* — usually 3sg or 3pl:

| infinitive | class | 3sg subj | 3pl subj | with `թող` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| գալ "come" | II | գա | գան | `Թող գա` / `Թող գան` |
| լինել "be" | I | լինի | լինեն | `Թող լինի` |
| անել "do" | I | անի | անեն | `Թող անի` |
| մտնել "enter" | I | մտնի | մտնեն | `Թող մտնեն` |
| մնալ "stay" | II | մնա | մնան | `Թող մնա` |

Negation is the ordinary `չ-` prefix on the subjunctive
(`Թող չգա` "let him not come"). `թող` scopes over the whole
clause, so material can intervene between it and the verb [#2]:

> `Թող այդ մասին ոչ ոք չիմանա։` — "Let no one find out about it."

## Attested paradigm (parnasyan, Russian `пусть` glosses)

Parnasyan p260 lays the construction out with Russian equivalents
[#1]:

| Armenian | Russian | English |
|---|---|---|
| `Թող լինի միշտ արևը` | `Пусть всегда будет солнце!` | "Let the sun always shine" |
| `Թող ներս մտնեն` | `Пусть войдут` | "Let them come in" |
| `Թող այսօր չգա` | `Пусть сегодня (он) не приходит` | "Let him not come today" |
| `Թող ասեն` | `Пусть скажут` | "Let them say (it)" |

The Russian gloss is the load-bearing disambiguator. `пусть` +
finite verb is unambiguously a 3rd-person directive, which locks
the reading of the Armenian: `Թող գա` is **"let him come"**, not
the 2sg imperative "come!" (that would be `արի`). Tioyan makes the
unexpressed subject explicit with a parenthetical [#3]:

> `Թող գա: Пусть (он) придет.` — "Let him come."

## The `մնա` ambiguity (why this topic exists)

For class-II (*-ալ*) verbs, **two forms collapse to one string**:

- **2sg imperative** `մնա՛` "stay!" (bare stem)
- **3sg subjunctive** `(որ/թող) մնա` "(that) it stay"

Identical on the page. The disambiguator is the frame, not the
word. After `թող`, `մնա` can only be the **3sg subjunctive**,
because `թող` selects a subjunctive and builds a 3rd-person wish:

> `Թող մնա մյուս անգամ։` — "Let it stay for another time."

Here the subject of `մնա` is an unstated *it* (the matter being
deferred), 3rd person. This is **not** the addressee-directed
"stay!" imperative, and there is no 2nd-person "you" and no verb
of *leaving* in the Armenian — a transparent gloss is *"let it
remain / let it wait,"* not *"[you] leave it."* (Source: the
Dav/Vnas line is a productive instance of the attested pattern;
`Թող մնա` itself is a corpus gap — see frontmatter.)

## Register

Attested across registers: didactic-standard (Parnasyan p260–263,
Tioyan p217) and Yerevan-colloquial (Ghamoyan p67: `ինչ ուզում ա,
թող անի` "whatever he wants, let him do it"; `թող գնա` "let him
go") [#4]. It is the ordinary, register-neutral way to form a
3rd-person directive.

## Contrastive notes

**For an English L1**: maps cleanly onto *let him / let it / let
them …*. The trap is treating the Armenian verb as 2nd-person
(because `մնա`, `գա` look like imperatives) — it agrees with the
3rd-person subject of the wish, not with the hearer.

**For a Russian L1**: `թող` ≈ `пусть` (the parnasyan glosses make
this explicit). Both take a finite verb agreeing with the 3rd-person
subject; neither uses an infinitive.
