# Currency exchange — vocabulary, verbs, and case-frame

Armenian has a small family of `փոխ-` ("change / swap") verbs and
nouns that cover what English handles with "exchange / convert /
change / swap / transfer." Choosing the right one for a service-
encounter sentence requires distinguishing **what each verb's
argument frame is** — including the surprise that the most
obvious candidate, `փոխանակել` "to exchange," is *not* a
transitive "trade X for Y" verb in Armenian.

## The `փոխ-` family

| word | gloss | frame |
|---|---|---|
| `փոխանակել` (vb) / `փոխանակում` (n) | exchange (reciprocal / symmetric) | single primary argument (acc on verb, gen on noun) |
| `փոխել` (vb) | change, swap, (currency) exchange | acc-source; "for Y" adjunct as with other transfer verbs (frame inherited, not corpus-attested for `փոխել` specifically) |
| `փոխարկել` (vb) / `փոխարկում` (n) | convert (between currency types) | acc-source + dative-target (web-attested) |
| `փոխանցել` (vb) / `փոխանցում` (n) | transfer (between accounts) | acc-source + dative-target |
| `վերածել` (vb) | turn-into, convert-into | acc-source + dative-target |

The four `-ակ-`/`-ել` verbs aren't synonyms. The semantic split
is **what changes hands** vs. **what changes form**:

- **`փոխանակել` / `փոխանակում`** — *parties exchange* something
  (mutual, reciprocal). [#1]
- **`փոխանցել` / `փոխանցում`** — *something is transferred* from
  one location/account to another (same kind of thing throughout). [#6]
- **`փոխարկել` / `փոխարկում`** / **`վերածել`** — *something is
  converted* from one type to another (currency, units). Not in
  the four-book corpus; web-attested only.
- **`փոխել`** — generic *change/swap*; the wildcard. Parnasyan's
  glossary explicitly extends its sense to `обменивать` ("to
  exchange [currency]"). [#4]

## `փոխանակել` is single-argument

Counter-intuitive for an English/Russian speaker. The only
contextualised use of `փոխանակել` in the four-book corpus —
Sakayan p426 — is reciprocal [#1]:

> `հայացքներ փոխանակելով՝ փսփսում էին։`
> "Exchanging glances, they whispered."

The plural subject (`they`) exchange glances *with each other*.
There is no "in exchange for Y" argument slot. The same pattern
holds in contemporary written Armenian — SkyLabs (Armenian crypto
exchange) uses `արժույթների փոխանակում` "exchange of currencies"
(plural genitive), not `<X> + <Y>-target` (web evidence; see
"Contemporary register" below).

**Consequence for service-encounter usage**: to express "exchange
X for Y" with `փոխանակել`, the construction is

> `<X-gen> + փոխանակում` (nominal) — "exchange of X"
> `<X-acc> + փոխանակել` (verbal) — "to exchange X"

… with the "for Y" added as an *adjunct*, not absorbed into the
verb's argument frame.

## "In exchange for X" — the `<X-gen> դիմաց` postposition

The adjunct that delivers the "for Y" reading is **`<Y-gen> +
դիմաց`** — `դիմաց` is a postposition that takes a genitive and
means "in exchange for / against / opposite." Sakayan p443 [#2]:

> `զավակներին՝ մի քանի արծաթ դրամի դիմաց`
> "(traded the children) for a few silver coins."

The adjunct slots onto any verb of transfer or trade — and is
register-flexible: Sakayan's literary 19th-c register and
contemporary financial journalism use it identically (see
"Contemporary register").

## Pricing and amounts

Sakayan p117 is the corpus's most direct service-encounter
dialogue [#3]:

> `Ի՞նչ է վերջնական գինը։ — Շատ էժան է՝ Տասը հազար դրամ։`
> "What's the final price? — Very cheap, 10,000 drams."

Pattern: **numeral + `դրամ`** (bare, no plural, no case on the
currency-noun in citation form). The numeral-quantified currency
NP is the standard amount-expression.

## Exchange-rate vocabulary

Two terms, both attested, both Russian-loanword-based:

- **`կուրս`** (from Russian `курс`) — colloquial. Ghamoyan p109
  parenthesises `(դրամի)` to disambiguate from `կոնկուրս`
  "competition" [#7].
- **`արտարժույթի կուրս`** — formal/financial. Tioyan p257 has
  `Արտարժույթի կուրսի կտրուկ …` "(a sharp change in) the foreign-
  currency rate" [#5]. The full phrase stacks two genitive
  modifiers (`արտարժույթի` "of-foreign-currency" + `կուրսի`
  "of-rate"), normal Armenian syntax.

**Asking the rate at a counter**: combine `Ի՞նչ է` (Sakayan
p117 [#3]) with `կուրս` (Ghamoyan p109 [#7]) →

> `Ի՞նչ է դոլարի կուրսը այսօր։`
> "What's the dollar rate today?"

Substitute any currency in the genitive: `եվրոյի կուրսը`,
`ռուբլու կուրսը`, `USDT-ի կուրսը`. Standard formula.

Contrast: there is no `տարադրամ` "foreign currency" in the
corpus. The compound is real (user observation, Yerevan exchange-
bureau street signs say `Տարադրամի փոխանակում` "Foreign-currency
exchange"; see `walks/2026-05-11-currency-exchange-construction.md`)
but the four pedagogical sources happen to use only `արտարժույթ`.
Both are productive.

## Contemporary register: cryptocurrency

The four corpus books all predate widespread cryptocurrency
adoption. Crypto-domain Armenian is **web-attested only** and
extends the corpus-grounded constructions above into a new
lexical domain. Marked clearly as off-corpus.

### Bitcoin orthography is not settled

Three variants coexist on contemporary Armenian sites:

- **`բիթքոյն`** — Bitcoin.org Armenian translation
- **`բիթկոյն`** — B24.am financial press
- **`բիթքոին`** — Hetq.am investigative journalism

All transliterate English *Bitcoin*; the variation is in whether
the `tc` cluster maps to `թք` (aspirated `t` + `kh`) or `տկ`
(unaspirated `t` + `k`), and whether the final diphthong is
`-ոյն` or `-ոին`. No consensus. A learner should expect to read
all three.

### Register split: `կրիպտոարժույթ` vs. `կրիպտոակտիվ`

- **`կրիպտոարժույթ`** "cryptocurrency" — general / casual. Used
  on Wikipedia, Hetq, B24, SkyLabs.
- **`կրիպտոակտիվ`** "crypto-asset" — legal-formal. The 2022
  Armenian law uses this term: *ՀՀ Օրենքը Կրիպտոակտիվների
  Մասին* ("RA Law on Crypto-Assets"). Substitutes the
  Russian-loanword `արժույթ` "currency" with the Latinate
  `ակտիվ` "asset" to mark legal-financial register.

### Verb frames in real crypto-domain Armenian

The web-attested usage extends `id#1-#4` directly:

| English | Armenian | source |
|---|---|---|
| "Buy Bitcoin" | `բիթքոին գնել` / `Գնեք Բիթքոյն` | Bitcoin.org Armenian |
| "Sell BTC" | `վաճառել BTC` | Hetq |
| "Acquire Bitcoin" (formal) | `բիթքոինի ձեռք բերելու եղանակը` | Hetq (note **genitive** under nominalised verb) |
| "Convert into Bitcoin" | `Վերածել բիթքոինի ներդրումի` | Hetq (note **dative target** `բիթքոինի`) |
| "Convert (amount)" | `գումարը, որը ցանկանում եք փոխարկել` | Hetq |
| "Exchange of currencies" | `արժույթների փոխանակում` | SkyLabs |
| "In exchange for crypto" | `Կրիպտոյի դիմաց ստանում է դոլար կամ դրամ` | MediaFactory.am |

The MediaFactory passage **independently confirms** the
`<X-gen> + դիմաց` construction (id#2 above) in a contemporary
financial-journalism register, 150+ years removed from Sakayan's
literary source. The construction is settled standard.

### Operational sentences for a learner

Reconstructed from corpus-grounded frames + web-attested vocab.
Rows marked **(frag.)** are infinitival or nominal formulae —
the kind you'd see on signs, in apps, or as topic-of-conversation
NPs. At a counter, finitise with `ուզում եմ …` ("I want to …") or
imperative `…եք` (addressed to the teller):

| English | Armenian | shape |
|---|---|---|
| "I want to buy USDT" | `Ուզում եմ USDT գնել։` | full |
| "I want to buy USDT with drams" | `Ուզում եմ USDT գնել դրամով։` | full (`-ով` = means/manner) |
| "Convert 100,000 drams to USDT" | `100 000 դրամը փոխարկեք USDT-ի։` | imperative — addressed to the teller |
| "Convert 100,000 drams to USDT" (infinitive) | `100 000 դրամ USDT-ի փոխարկել` | **(frag.)** |
| "Exchange of drams for USDT" | `Դրամի փոխանակում USDT-ի դիմաց` | **(frag.)** — nominal, like signage |
| "Sell BTC for drams" | `Ուզում եմ BTC վաճառել դրամի դիմաց։` | full |
| "How much will I get for 1000 USDT?" | `1000 USDT-ի դիմաց որքա՞ն կստանամ։` | full |

The instrumental `-ով` marks the **form/medium** of payment, not
the target currency of an exchange. `կանխիկ դրամով վճարել` "pay
in cash (drams)" is web- and Bayramyan-attested; `<currency>-ով
փոխանակել` (instrumental as exchange-target) is **not** attested
anywhere and should be avoided.

## Contrastive notes

**For an English L1**: English "exchange X for Y" is transitive
with a prepositional adjunct (`for Y`). Armenian splits this:
the verb (`փոխանակել`) takes only `X`; the `Y` rides on a
postpositional adjunct (`Y-gen դիմաց`). Translating the English
two-argument frame piece-by-piece into Armenian produces an
ungrammatical sentence; the construction is reorganised.

**For a Russian L1**: Russian `обменивать X на Y` has `на Y`
where Armenian has `Y-ի դիմաց`. The semantic mapping is direct;
the case-marking differs (`на` + acc in Russian; gen + `դիմաց`
in Armenian). For *conversion* (`превратить X в Y` / `конвертировать
X в Y`), Armenian uses `Y-ի փոխարկել` / `Y-ի վերածել` (dative
target), parallel in structure but again with case rather than
preposition.

**False-friend on instrumental**: Russian `платить рублями` /
`оплатить рублями` (instrumental of currency-form) maps to
Armenian `դրամով վճարել` *only* in the manner-of-payment reading.
The Russian pattern does **not** extend to currency exchange in
either language — `обменивать долларами` is also ungrammatical
in Russian; the trap is symmetric.

**Minimal pair `փոխել` vs. `փոխարկել`** — the false-friend a
learner most often hits because bilingual dictionaries gloss
both as "change / convert":

> `Փոխեցի 100 դոլարը։` "I changed the 100 dollars."
> (generic swap — could be bill-for-bill, denominations,
> currency type left unspecified by the verb.)
>
> `Փոխարկեցի 100 դոլարը դրամի։` "I converted the 100 dollars
> to drams." (typed conversion — currency type explicitly
> changed; the dative target is grammatically required for
> the conversion reading to land.)

Use `փոխարկել` when the *kind* of money is changing; `փոխել`
covers anything looser.

## Pragmatic notes

- **At a physical exchange bureau**: Yerevan street signs use
  the nominal formula `Տարադրամի փոխանակում`. Walking up and
  asking `Հարյուր դոլարի փոխանակում, խնդրեմ։` ("100 dollars'
  exchange, please") is the idiomatic short request — it
  mirrors the signage and minimises the case-marking the
  learner has to produce live.
- **At a crypto-terminal / app**: the imperative-of-app pattern
  (`Գնեք Բիթքոյն` "Buy Bitcoin" on Bitcoin.org Armenian) is
  the canonical UI register. `Մուտքագրեք գումարը, որը ցանկանում
  եք փոխարկել` "Enter the amount you wish to convert" (Hetq) is
  representative of guided-flow Armenian.
- **`մանրել`** "to break (a bill) into smaller units" is a
  distinct construction — Bayramyan p63 phrasebook: `Մանրեք,
  խնդրեմ, դոլարը 50 դրամի` "Break the dollar into 50-dram
  [bills], please" (2pl-formal imperative, dative on target
  denomination). Not in the four-book corpus.
