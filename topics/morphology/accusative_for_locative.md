# Accusative-for-locative case substitution on place names

In Yerevan colloquial Armenian, the **bare / accusative form** of a
place name is widely used in stative-locative contexts where literary
Armenian requires the **locative case** (`-ում`, ներգոյական
հոլով).

## The phenomenon, in ghamoyan's words

Ghamoyan p87 documents the pattern with explicit metalinguistic
framing [#1]:

> «Շատ տարա[ծ]ված են տեղի իմաստով հայցական հոլովի
> կիռառություններ ներգոյականի փոխարեն: Օրինակ՝
> *Ես Երևան եմ ապրում* ... Կամ՝ *Էս տարի Քոբուլեթի
> հանգստացա* ...» *([ծ] inserted: corpus has OCR-error
> `տարաված`; the intended word is `տարածված` "widespread,
> common")*

> "Very widespread are uses of the accusative case with
> locative meaning, in place of the locative. For example:
> *I live in Yerevan*. Or: *This year I vacationed in
> Kobuleti*."

The structural claim: for inanimate nouns, the Armenian accusative
is syncretic with the nominative (both are the bare-stem form, no
case-ending). So when ghamoyan calls this "accusative-for-locative,"
the **surface output is the bare form** — what an English speaker
would describe as "the locative-`-ում` is just dropped."

(*A note on ghamoyan's second example: `Քոբուլեթի հանգստացա` "I
vacationed in Kobuleti" has a stem-final `-ի` that might look like
gen/dat morphology, but isn't. Kobuleti is Georgian (ქობულეთი),
and Georgian place names ending in `-ი` (the Georgian nominative
suffix) are borrowed into Armenian preserving the vowel:
`Թբիլիսի`, `Բաթումի`, `Քութայիսի`, `Քոբուլեթի`. The bare
Armenian form of the toponym **is** `Քոբուլեթի`. For inanimate
nouns, accusative = nominative = this bare form. Ghamoyan's
"accusative" label is correct; the example reduces from literary
`Քոբուլեթիում` to colloquial `Քոբուլեթի` — same shape of
reduction as `Երևանում` → `Երևան`, just with a stem that
already ended in a vowel.*)

## The minimal pair

The shift is from one well-formed Armenian construction to another,
licensed in different registers:

| register | form | gloss |
|---|---|---|
| Literary-standard | `Ես Երևանում եմ ապրում։` | "I live in Yerevan." |
| Yerevan-colloquial [#1] | `Ես Երևան եմ ապրում։` | "I live in Yerevan." |

Same speaker can switch between them depending on context. The
Ghamoyan authors (Լուսինե Ղամոյան, Մերի Սարգսյան, Անահիտ
Քարտաշյան) themselves use the literary form `Երևանում` consistently
in academic prose ([#2], p21: `Երևանում այժմ գործում են երկու
տասնյակ պետական բարձրագույն...` "Twenty state higher [universities]
currently function in Yerevan") — confirming this is a **register
feature**, not a paradigm shift. The two forms coexist in the
speaker's repertoire.

## The bare form already has independent life in motion contexts

The motivation for the colloquial reduction is partly that **the
bare form is already standard in directional / motion-verb contexts
across all registers**. Sakayan's literary-standard textbook
presents this without register marking:

- `Ո՞ւր ես գնում։ Երևան եմ գնում։` (sak. p111 [#3]) "Where are you
  going? — I'm going to Yerevan." Bare `Երևան` + motion verb
  `գնալ`.
- `Մեր տուն գնացի՞ր։` (sak. p111) "Did you go to our home?" Bare
  `տուն` + motion verb.
- `Վերջապես տուն եկա։` (sak. p104) "I finally came home." Bare
  `տուն` + motion verb `գալ`.

So the bare form has been licensed in Armenian's directional /
endpoint slot since the literary norm. The Yerevan-colloquial
pattern **extends bare-form licensing from motion verbs to stative
verbs** — a verb-class boundary that the literary norm enforces but
the colloquial register has eroded.

## Cross-linguistic context

English and Russian both require a preposition for stative location
with a place name; neither has a parallel of the Armenian bare-form
reduction:

| | English | Russian | Armenian (literary) | Armenian (Yerevan colloq.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "I live in Yerevan" | live **in** Yerevan | живу **в** Ереване (LOC) | `Երևան-ում եմ ապրում` | `Երևան եմ ապրում` [#1] |

Where the L1s do have a comparable reduction is on the single lexeme
*home*: English `I'm home` (bare-noun adverbial) vs. literary
`I'm at home`; Russian `Я дома` (adverbial-form) vs. `Я в доме`.
Armenian's Yerevan-colloquial pattern extends the same *kind* of
case-flattening reduction across the open class of place names,
rather than lexicalising it on a single noun.

## Pragmatic / register notes

- The construction is **register-marked**: the ghamoyan authors
  present it as a colloquial feature and use the standard locative
  themselves when writing academically. Speakers retain both forms
  and choose by context.
- The two verbs cited by ghamoyan are `ապրել` "live" (stative
  habitual) and `հանգստանալ` "rest, vacation" (bounded-stative). On
  this N=2 sample one might guess the reduction favours
  stative/habitual aspect, but the range of licensing verbs isn't
  surveyed in any of the four source books — flagged in gaps.
- The reduction is consistent with the broader case-system
  flexibility documented in Yerevan-colloquial speech. Compare
  `topics/phonology/yerevan_consonant_reductions.md` for parallel
  phonological reductions and `topics/morphology/colloquial_copula_a.md`
  for the colloquial copula `ա` (← literary `է`) that often
  co-occurs in the same register.

## Open questions worth testing in spoken data

Three predictions / open questions, ordered by how far they extend
beyond what ghamoyan cites:

1. **Lexical extension to other place names**: would `Մոսկվա եմ
   ապրում` "I live in Moscow" be heard? Same construction as the
   attested `Երևան եմ ապրում`, just substituting a different
   place-name token. Probably yes if the pattern is productive;
   not in the four-book corpus.

2. **Pure copular reduction**: would `Երևան եմ` "I'm in Yerevan"
   (no stative verb, just copula) be heard? One step further
   reduction than [#1]; plausibly the most likely environment to
   look for it would be a phone call ("where are you?" — "Yerevan
   ա" / "Երևան եմ"). Not in the four-book corpus.

3. **Common-noun extension** (`տուն եմ`, `դպրոց եմ`, `գործ եմ`):
   genuinely open. The reasoning that bare `տուն` already has
   motion-verb licensing (`տուն եկա` sak. p104) might extend to
   stative — but the workspace's documented failure mode
   (`errors/2026-05-09-004-sov-gal-uncited-extrapolation.md`)
   warns against extending a cited pattern to uncited members
   without independent evidence. Place-name extension (1) is a
   safer prediction; common-noun extension is a hypothesis that
   could go either way.

If the colloquial reduction is genuinely productive across all
proper and common nouns with locative meaning, the predicted forms
should be readily heard in Yerevan-colloquial speech. If only
proper place names (or even only specific place names) license it,
the pattern is more lexically restricted than ghamoyan's `շատ
տարածված` ("very widespread") framing suggests.
